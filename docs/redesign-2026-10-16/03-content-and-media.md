# Content, media and programming

## Content strategy

Build a curated core, not an undifferentiated thousand-exercise dump. Broad database access is useful for sourcing; the user-facing selection needs consistent naming, accurate equipment, reviewed instruction, good media and understandable progression.

Proposed full-redesign inventory: **90 movements = 72 strength + 12 mobility + 6 conditioning**, **24 standalone workout templates**, and **six program structures**. These are editorial scope targets, not evidence-based ideal category quotas or promises that all supplier assets exist. Keep the existing 30 IDs and history. Publish new items only when their metadata, media/fallback and review gates pass. Expand beyond 90 after usage reveals actual gaps.

The first content milestone can ship 48 reviewed movements and eight excellent workouts while the remainder is produced. That is an intermediate milestone, not completion of the full requested redo. Maintain visible coverage counts in the implementation ledger.

## Catalog quality and discovery rules

- Separate broad browse categories from precise muscle IDs and movement patterns. Support horizontal/vertical push and pull, squat, hinge, lunge/step, elbow flexion/extension, carry, anti-extension, anti-rotation and rotation.
- Provide granular equipment requirements and alternative configurations. The availability predicate must check **all** required pieces. Bodyweight is a loading type, not proof no equipment is needed.
- Store measurement kinds such as reps+load, reps-only, duration, duration+load, distance+load and distance+time. Reps per side and alternating total reps are distinct.
- Store load semantics: per dumbbell, combined external load, added bodyweight load, or assistance. Do not silently change semantics for legacy logs.
- A substitution has an explicit reviewed relationship and reason. Shared muscle category alone is insufficient. When no safe matching alternative is available, say so and allow skipping or changing the workout.
- Filtered discovery defaults to useful coverage; favorites/recently used are personalized shortcuts. An A–Z mode is deterministic. Random variation is not program progression.
- Each exercise receives aliases, difficulty/setup complexity, concise cue, setup, steps, common errors and modification links. Review broad warnings in context; never claim an exercise treats a diagnosed injury.
- Treat provider IDs as external references. Kinset IDs remain stable; supplier removal or replacement must not destroy history. Separate user-entered records and owned descriptions from expiring supplier metadata.

## Six program structures

| Program | Proposed structure | Programming requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Start strong | 4 weeks, 2–3 days/week | Foundation A/B rotation; equipment-specific alternatives; no random daily shuffle |
| Dumbbell strength | 6 weeks, 3 days/week | Stable reviewed exercise families, volume/rest progression and explicit repeat-week option |
| Gym foundations | 6 weeks, 3 days/week | Equipment orientation, full-body gym days, planned progression |
| Upper / lower | 6 weeks, 4 days/week | Two distinct upper and lower sessions; avoid overcounting duplicate templates |
| Strength + running | 6 weeks, configurable strength/run days | Reviewed scheduling/recovery logic; manual running supported until GPS passes native gates |
| Move consistently | 4 weeks, short sessions | Flexible strength, mobility and walking schedule; respects rest and rescheduling |

These structures reuse versioned templates intentionally. A program stores weeks, sessions, progression policy, planned rest days, enrollment version and schedule overrides. Frequency is chosen within the program's supported range, not forced into the existing 2–4-day profile enum. Begin with transparent, reviewer-approved progression rules; present suggestions with a reason and accept/decline controls. Never silently increase an active session's loads.

## Media decision

Use short looping video as the preferred presentation format, with posters and text fallbacks. GIF is an acceptable supplied format but not the desired product constraint: the player needs pause, seek, speed and efficient variants. Transcoding/cropping is permitted only when the license allows it. Keep the film visible in context; muscle education is a separate layer.

| Option | Fit | Constraint / recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Commissioned or negotiated downloadable human clips** | Best control of quality, identity and offline instruction | Preferred end-state; requires production budget, model releases, technique review, and explicit distribution/derivative rights. No quote obtained. |
| **MuscleWiki API** | Strong shortlist for online filmed instruction, multiple views and structured data | Paid commercial plans; assess 12 representative movements before selecting. Standard terms make it an online option, not an offline library. |
| **Gym Visual** | Broad purchasable exercise animation/video catalog | Candidate if authored animation meets quality bar and exact use is covered; still visually animated, so do not assume it resolves the user's mannequin concern. |
| **ExerciseDB / AscendAPI** | Structured exercise/GIF integration | Standard storage restrictions are a poor fit for durable offline packs. The free version is not a commercial content shortcut. |
| **wger** | Open exercise infrastructure/content candidate | Useful for selective sourcing; asset-level quality and attribution/share-alike review required. No assumption of uniform premium filmed coverage. |

MuscleWiki advertises paid commercial use and a $10/1,000-call testing plan; Growth $39.99/30,000 calls; Professional $99.99/100,000 calls, with bodymaps and more muscle metadata on the higher tier. Prices are observed October 16, not a purchase or fixed operating budget. [Pricing](https://api.musclewiki.com/pricing), [capabilities](https://api.musclewiki.com/).

Its October 8 terms allow text caching up to 30 days, thumbnails/bodymaps up to 24 hours, and video playback buffers only. Offline video storage is prohibited. Branding must remain visible; a legal disclosure is required. A transparent server proxy without stored/cached video, or supported media tokens, protects the API key. Range requests can consume quota. Verify ambiguous thumbnail language and required rights with the vendor. [API terms](https://api.musclewiki.com/api-terms).

Gym Visual's license describes perpetual commercial use of purchased non-watermarked media in apps, with restrictions on redistribution/resale and AI modification. Obtain written clarification for a paid subscription app, protected offline packs and permitted conversion; do not treat public thumbnails as licensed. Its catalog advertises $6/video after five items and $0.90/GIF after ten; 90 qualifying videos would be roughly $540 before tax/extra angles/custom terms, not a vendor quote. [License](https://gymvisual.com/content/9-license), [catalog pricing](https://gymvisual.com/13-media-content).

AscendAPI's published ExerciseDB terms restrict persistent metadata/media storage and operational caching beyond one hour. The free V1 documentation specifies non-commercial use. Similarly named ExerciseDB websites are not interchangeable sources of rights. [Terms](https://exercisedb.notion.site/ExerciseDB-API-Terms-of-Use-226983b728ca8090bf7be79564e4b356), [free dataset documentation](https://oss.exercisedb.dev/docs).

wger distinguishes AGPL application code from CC BY-SA initial content; evaluate individual media and attribution rather than assuming every file shares the same license. [Project documentation](https://wger.readthedocs.io/en/stable/).

**Decision:** build a provider-independent player and owned exercise IDs now. Prefer downloadable rights for the core collection. If the owner selects MuscleWiki for speed, label video as online and make all locally licensed instructions/logging work without it. Do not put its video through the current generic service-worker cache. Do not buy multiple libraries merely to maximize a headline count.

## Supplier evaluation protocol

Evaluate squat, RDL, floor press, incline push-up, row, pulldown, shoulder press, curl, triceps extension, dead bug, side plank and unilateral lunge. Check exact variation/equipment, full-body framing, technique, smooth complete repetitions, left/right cues, visual consistency, small-screen legibility and poster quality. Score quality/coverage/rights/offline/performance/cost separately. Reject unclear technique or insufficient rights regardless of composite score.

Unresolved procurement questions: offline device storage; commercial paid-app rights; CDN/proxy rules; watermark/crop/transcode rules; durable internal ID mapping and history labels; export rights; license survival after cancellation; replacement/removal procedure; instructor/model releases; service availability; quotas including media range requests. Get exact answers before production sourcing, not by repeatedly rediscovering vendors.

Operating cost estimate should use measured calls/session and media bytes/session × active usage, plus storage/CDN, map tiles and backend. A monthly plan price alone cannot predict cost. Do a bounded sample integration and usage trace before committing.

## Media and review contract

Store `provider`, `providerExerciseId`, `providerAssetId`, `licenseId`, `rightsCheckedAt`, `expiresAt`, `deliveryMode` (owned/stream-only), `allowedCacheSeconds`, `offlineAllowed`, `transformationsAllowed`, `attribution`, `poster`, video variants and fallback. Do not persist expiring signed URLs as permanent identifiers.

Player states: poster → loading → ready/playing/paused; plus offline, missing, expired, failed and restricted. Logging never awaits media. Retry is bounded; a failed video keeps the exercise name, cues and muscle text usable. Use muted inline playback only after the relevant view opens; reduced-motion/data-saver users get a poster until play. Stop offscreen/hidden media. Captions/transcript are required for meaningful narrated audio.

Content workflow: draft → technical validation → technique review → rights approval → published → deprecated. Record reviewer, date, version and reasons. Provider additions do not auto-publish. Deprecation hides an exercise from new discovery but preserves historical records. Every curated workout is rehearsed for real duration and setup transitions; every core movement has reviewed instruction even if video cannot load.
